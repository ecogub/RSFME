# M4c-4: Results ↔ Discussion Repetition Analysis

Three areas where the Discussion restates Results observations rather than adding interpretive value.

## 1. Aulenbach method yields "defensible" selection

**Results** (para 140, after Fig 7 discussion):
> "It should be noted that the Aulenbach et al. 2016 selection method does still select a defensible method (linear interpolation or averaging, depending on sampling frequency), without the benefit of referring to the high-frequency time series."

**Discussion** (para 169, opening of "Insights on load estimation uncertainty"):
> "Using our simplified application of Aulenbach and others' (2016) method selection procedure yields a defensible selection of load estimation methods."

**Recommendation:** Keep the Discussion version (it's the interpreting context) and cut the Results sentence. The Results should present what happened; "defensible" is a judgment that belongs in Discussion.

---

## 2. LI/composite leverage autocorrelation → best for dense data

**Results** (para 140):
> "When data frequency was high, both concentrations and model residuals were highly autocorrelated. As expected, under these conditions, methods that leverage autocorrelation, such as linear interpolation and composite, outperform all others."

**Discussion** (para 169):
> "densely sampled data is usually best estimated by leveraging highly autocorrelated rating residuals (composite method) or highly autocorrelated concentrations (linear interpolation)"

**Recommendation:** The Results version describes the observation; the Discussion version generalizes it. Both are fine — the overlap is minor and natural. No change needed.

---

## 3. No strong C:Q → use linear interpolation

**Results** (para 146, after Fig 8):
> "Generally, linear interpolation performed best for this solute year, regardless of sampling frequency. This is again contrary to what the Aulenbach et al. 2016 method would suggest..."

**Discussion** (para 174):
> "Our coarsening experiments also reinforce previous findings that generally, when there is not a strong C:Q relationship, users should rely on linear interpolation or averaging methods."

**Recommendation:** The Discussion version adds the "reinforce previous findings" framing, which is appropriate. The Results version could be tightened to just state the observation without the Aulenbach comparison (move that to Discussion). But this is a judgment call — the repetition is mild.

---

## Overall Assessment

The repetition is less severe than the M0 review suggested. The Discussion does add interpretive framing in most cases. The one clear cut is **item 1** — remove the "defensible" judgment from Results. The others are borderline and could be left as-is for a first submission.

**Action:** Apply item 1 only. Flag items 2–3 for Nic's judgment during final read-through.
